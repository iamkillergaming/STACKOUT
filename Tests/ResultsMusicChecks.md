`ResultsMusicChecks.cs` verifies results audio in the authored SampleScene. Copy it into an isolated project's `Assets` after running `OriginalSpriteSetup.BatchSetup`, then run Unity with `-batchmode -force-glcore -projectPath <isolated-project> -executeMethod ResultsMusicChecks.Run -logFile <log>`. Omit `-quit`: the fixture enters Play mode and exits itself. Success prints `RESULTS_MUSIC_CHECKS_PASSED`; setup or coroutine failures exit with code 1. It saves only `Assets/ResultsMusicChecks.unity` in the isolated copy.

The fixture validates the exact supplied `05. Result (Win).mp3` and `06. Result (Eliminated).mp3` assignments and confirms that MatchFlow observes the actual saved death and victory sources. Five small rounds use saved opponents and the normal READY/countdown/GO flow. Three controlled rounds check that a player elimination keeps music stopped during the death sweep and a longer custom audio cue; the eliminated-results track then loops while spectating and continues without restarting when the last surviving opponent wins. A player victory must wait for its custom victory cue. Both original result tracks must advance their AudioSource sample positions across repeated updates. Retrying after each result restores menu music; retrying during another victory cue must cancel that cue without later replacing the lobby track.

Two additional rounds restore the real `me_game_gameover.wav` and `me_game_clear.wav` cues and let them finish naturally. The handoff must preserve their audible portions while skipping their long silent tails. Each supplied result MP3 must start near its authored 1.5-second offset, have loaded nonzero decoded samples there, and produce nonzero `AudioSource.GetOutputData` samples within 0.8 seconds. The fixture logs the observed handoff and output RMS as `RESULTS_AUDIO_SIGNAL`. Stopping each result source must recover playback on the following updates and continue advancing. The handoff wait has an eight-second timeout for each outcome.

Only the isolated runtime fixture shortens countdown/death-sweep timings and pauses gameplay. The first three rounds substitute a silent thirty-second death/victory cue and mute sources to make the gates observable. They temporarily shorten the original-cue timing to 0.01 seconds, proving that custom cues remain protected after that deadline. The final two rounds restore the saved cue timings, keep the real cue sources at zero volume, and set music volume to 0.05, so they verify actual source output without loud cues. Results, menu, and battle tracks always retain their original assets. These checks require functioning Unity audio and fail if source output remains silent; they do not establish physical speaker output or subjective mix balance.
