`TargetingWarningChecks.cs` exercises the authored targeting warning in an isolated copy of the final Unity project. Copy the check into that copy's `Assets` directory, then run Unity with `-batchmode -force-glcore -screen-width 1920 -screen-height 1080 -projectPath <isolated-project> -executeMethod TargetingWarningChecks.Run -logFile <log>`. Do not use `-quit` or `-nographics`: it enters Play mode, performs lifecycle checks, and renders a 1920×1080 PNG. Success logs `TARGETING_WARNING_CHECKS_PASSED` and exits zero.

The supplied SampleScene must contain one saved opponent plus the authored warning frame and 392 targeting-segment sprites (four per opponent). The fixture adds two saved bot-prefab instances in slots 49 and 97, initially inactive, and saves only `Assets/TargetingWarningChecks.unity` in the isolated project. It never saves the original scene or prefabs. Gravity and automatic bot placement are held while actual target mappings are controlled deterministically.

Targeting audio checks first validate the saved dedicated 2D AudioSource and its `Assets/General Sounds/se_game_target.wav` reference, then temporarily substitute a ten-second silent clip. Actual `AudioSource.isPlaying` probes verify a new live incoming attacker plays a cue. The fixture stops the source and refreshes in the same frame to prove unchanged, reordered, duplicated, removed, disabled, or eliminated attackers cannot replay it. Outgoing selection/routes, stack height, and queued garbage cannot cue it either. Batched additions announce the changed set, subsequent refreshes remain silent, and leaving/rejoining announces again. Playback must stop when the final attacker leaves, on pause, player/presentation/arena disable, victory, or player death. Resuming or re-enabling the same retained set stays silent; a cleared arena or new round announces its restored attackers. These playback probes verify batched notification and suppression, rather than counting DSP voices within one call. The authored source must retain its identity, and the original clip is restored before exit. Do not disable Unity audio for this fixture.

Coverage: an empty-board 1v1 shows WATCH OUT and one incoming line; a high stack, an outgoing selection, or an old pending garbage packet without a current attacker shows neither. Each attacker must use a continuous prefix of one to four yellow segments, with every unused trailing sprite disabled. The route must join the warning's capsule outline to the correct miniature edge. Left/right attackers must attach to the matching rounded warning end rather than a fixed bottom-center anchor. Every segment must avoid only the actual main 10×20 playfield, expanded by the visible stroke's half-width and a small clearance. Decorative board artwork, borders, and side panels are allowed. A positive 1v1 regression requires the route to cross cabinet decoration outside the warning footprint while still clearing the playable grid, preventing the old overly wide cabinet detour from returning. The fixture moves an attacker between existing top/bottom slots on both sides to verify that routes update without crossing the playable grid or changing the camera. A temporary attacker below the cabinet must use one unobstructed direct segment; moving it back must rebuild the appropriate route without stale segments. Retargeting, miniature movement, bot KO/unregistration/re-registration, win, player pause/disable, presentation/arena disable, player death, and both restart paths remove or rebuild the expected lines. The authored warning, all 392 segment renderers, board cells/frames, TMP labels, and camera background/position/rotation/zoom/aspect must retain their identities or values.

The screenshots are `/tmp/tetris-targeting-warning.png` (three live attacking miniature boards, each with four actual AI placements) and `/tmp/tetris-targeting-warning-1v1.png` (the remaining real opponent and its single incoming targeting line). The fixture explicitly fits a 16:9 camera once before recording the unchanged-camera baseline. Rendering uses a 1080p render texture, submits a StandardRequest when a scriptable render pipeline is active, and restores the camera target/aspect afterward. Targeting itself may not reframe the camera. A working graphics context is required so both PNGs can be visually inspected. Coroutine exceptions exit with code 1 instead of leaving the batch process waiting.
